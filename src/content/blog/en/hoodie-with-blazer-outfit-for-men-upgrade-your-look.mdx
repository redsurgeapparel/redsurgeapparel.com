**A hoodie under a blazer looks good when the hoodie is thin and the blazer has no shoulder padding.** That's genuinely the whole formula. Everything that goes wrong with this outfit — and plenty does — comes down to one of those two pieces being too heavy or too structured.

If you've tried this look and it landed like you'd forgotten your suit jacket on a gym run, you probably grabbed a fleece. If it looked like you were wearing your dad's blazer over a t-shirt, the jacket was the problem. Both are fixable.

Here's the thing about the hoodie-blazer combination: it's been floating around menswear since Kanye started wearing it on red carpets in the late 2000s. Everyone agrees it looks cool. Almost nobody gets it right. The reason is almost always proportions.

So let me walk you through the two variables that decide everything, then give you seven combinations worth actually trying.

## The Two Variables That Decide Whether This Outfit Fails

Most hoodie-blazer attempts fall apart for one of two reasons.

**The hoodie is too heavy.** A thick fleece hoodie adds bulk under the arm and across the chest. What you actually want is a hoodie that feels closer to a heavy t-shirt or a fine-gauge sweater. If it could double as gym wear, it's too thick.

**The blazer is too structured.** A padded, slim-fit suit jacket fights the hoodie's softness. It wants to hold a shape; the hoodie wants to drape. You need a sport coat with minimal shoulder padding, a softer construction, and some surface texture — tweed, hopsack, corduroy, cotton-linen. Anything that reads as "formal suit" is going to lose the fight.

Get those two variables right and the outfit basically handles itself. Get them wrong and no amount of styling can save it.

## The Hoodie: Fabric, Fit, and the Pocket Problem

### Fabric weight matters more than anything else

| Fabric                       | Verdict | Why                                                      |
| ---------------------------- | ------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Merino wool**              | Best    | Thin, breathable, temperature-regulating, drapes cleanly |
| **High-quality Pima cotton** | Best    | Feels like a heavy tee, minimal bulk                     |
| **Mid-weight French terry**  | Good    | Works if the fit is slim                                 |
| **Heavy fleece / sherpa**    | Avoid   | Bulk, sausage arms, pulling buttons                      |
| **Shiny polyester**          | Avoid   | Looks cheap under a blazer                               |

If you only remember one thing from this guide, remember the top row. A merino hoodie is what makes this outfit work. It's thin enough to layer, warm enough for cool days, and it drapes instead of bulking up. Uniqlo sells a merino-blend hoodie every fall that's basically built for this. So does Everlane. You don't need to spend a lot — you need to spend _right_.

### Fit: slim to regular, hem at the hip

The hem is where most people mess up without realizing it. A hoodie under a blazer should hit at the hip — _slightly_ shorter than the blazer. If it hangs two or three inches below the jacket, it creates a messy horizontal line across your body and makes you look shorter than you are. Size down if you're between sizes; a tapered fit under a tailored jacket reads cleaner than a baggy one.

### The pocket rule

Kangaroo pockets are the enemy. A bulky front pocket bulging out from under the blazer buttons ruins the silhouette. Look for side-seam pockets — or, better, no pockets at all.

### Skip the tuck

Do not tuck your hoodie into your trousers. I know you've seen it on Pinterest. It creates a lump around the waist, pulls at the jacket, and looks worse than leaving it untucked. Every time.

### Hood structure isn't optional

A limp hood looks sad. You want one with some structure and a clean drape — double-layer hoods hold their shape better under a jacket collar. The hood should sit flat against your lapel, not bunch up under your chin.

## The Blazer: What to Actually Buy

### Construction

Unstructured or soft-shouldered, with minimal padding. Half-canvas construction gives you enough structure without stiffness. Natural shoulders that drape over the hood instead of jutting out above it.

### Fabric

Tweed, hopsack, corduroy, cotton-linen blends — anything with texture. Texture is what makes the casual hoodie feel intentional instead of sloppy. Avoid shiny suit fabrics and formal worsted wool.

### Fit

You're adding a layer underneath, so size up slightly or choose a blazer with a bit more room through the chest and waist. If the buttons pull, you went too tight.

## A Quick Personal Note

The first time I tried this combination, I wore a heavy grey Champion fleece under a slim navy suit jacket to a coffee meeting in the city. I spent the entire hour pulling at the buttons and folding my arms to hide the bulge under my chest. Not once did I feel like I'd nailed the look.

Now I own exactly one hoodie for layering — a merino one that weighs almost nothing — and the outfit works every time. Same formula, different variable. The difference between "trying the hoodie-blazer look" and "wearing it well" is basically one piece of clothing.

## 7 Hoodie + Blazer Outfit Formulas

### 1. The Monochrome Move (Foolproof)

**Charcoal grey hoodie + black unstructured blazer + black jeans + white sneakers.** Tonal contrast is subtle, the silhouette stays clean, and the white sneaker keeps it grounded. This is the easiest starting point if you've never tried the outfit before.

### 2. The Heritage Contrast

**Light grey marl hoodie + navy hopsack blazer + dark denim + brown suede boots or loafers.** The grey hoodie pops against the navy without shouting. J.Crew has been selling this exact combination for years — it's the entry point most guys start with.

### 3. The Earth-Tone Fall Fit

**Olive green hoodie + brown tweed blazer + charcoal chinos + leather boots.** Texture on texture. This one's for autumn and outdoor evening events when it's cold enough to justify the tweed.

### 4. The Zip-Up Smart Casual

**Fine-gauge zip hoodie (partially zipped) over a crisp white tee + unstructured charcoal blazer + tailored trousers + Chelsea boots.** The zip gives you a visible layering line and a cleaner neckline than a pullover. Especially good if you're self-conscious about your neck or jawline.

### 5. The Weekend Streetwear Fit

**Black pullover hoodie + oversized black blazer + straight-leg light-wash jeans + chunky white sneakers.** The oversized blazer balances the casual hoodie. Keep the hoodie plain — no large graphics.

### 6. The Travel Fit

**Navy merino hoodie + grey cotton-linen blazer + stretch chinos + clean minimal sneakers.** Breathable, layer-friendly, comfortable enough for a flight or a long day on your feet. This is the one I'd pack for a weekend trip.

### 7. The Date-Night Upgrade

**Black fine-knit hoodie + black velvet or textured blazer + black trousers + leather loafers.** All-black with texture contrast. The hoodie keeps it relaxed; the blazer makes it intentional. Reads dressy without actually being dressy.

## What Shoes to Wear (and What to Avoid)

**Wear:** White sneakers, Chelsea boots, leather loafers, suede desert boots.

**Skip:** Running shoes, heavily worn gym trainers, formal Oxfords. Oxfords clash with the hoodie's casual weight — the formality mismatch looks accidental, not intentional.

## What NOT to Do

- Heavy fleece under a slim suit jacket. Sausage arms.
- Tuck the hoodie in. Waist lump.
- A hoodie with a huge front pocket under a buttoned blazer.
- A padded, structured suit blazer with a casual hoodie.
- Large graphics or logos — they overwhelm the blazer's clean line.

## FAQ

### How do I wear a hoodie under a blazer without looking sloppy?

Use a thin merino wool or Pima cotton hoodie, an unstructured blazer, and a hem that hits at the hip. Avoid heavy fleece and padded suit jackets.

### What kind of hoodie should I wear under a blazer?

Merino wool, high-quality Pima cotton, or mid-weight French terry. The hoodie should feel like a heavy tee, not gym gear.

### Can I wear a zip-up hoodie under a blazer?

Yes. A fine-gauge zip hoodie partially zipped over a white tee gives you a cleaner neckline and a visible layering line.

### Should I tuck my hoodie into my trousers?

No. Tucking creates a lump around the waist. Leave the hoodie untucked and make sure the hem hits at the hip.

### What shoes go with a hoodie and blazer?

White sneakers, Chelsea boots, leather loafers, and suede desert boots. Avoid running shoes and formal Oxfords.
